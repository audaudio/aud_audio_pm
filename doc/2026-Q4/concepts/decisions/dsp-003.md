# dsp-003: The master limiter looks ahead 1.5 ms and never exceeds its ceiling

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-13
- Canonical source: tickets/2026-10-13-28-build-reverb-delay-and-a-master-limiter.md
  (scope 2, default 6); `src/aud_limiter_node.hpp` of `aud_dsp_effects`
- Refines: [graph-001](graph-001.md) — a node's latency on the output path
  delays live input and is reported; [app-001](app-001.md) — the limiter on
  the sum of the nodes
- Open work: a look-ahead the app can choose needs the engine to read the
  latency again after `prepare`; true-peak (inter-sample) limiting

## Decision

`aud.effects.limiter` is a look-ahead peak limiter of Audanika's own, after
Giannoulis, Massberg and Reiss (JAES 60(6), 2012) and Zölzer (DAFX, ch. 4).
It delays the signal by the look-ahead L = round(1.5 ms × sample rate)
frames and multiplies it with a gain that is linked over all channels: the
gain each frame needs to stay at the ceiling, the minimum of it over L + 1
frames, a release that only slows a rising gain, and a mean over L + 1
frames that turns every reduction into a ramp of L frames. Every peak meets
its full reduction, so no output sample exceeds `ceiling` (default −1 dBFS)
for any input; a final clamp holds the ceiling against rounding, and
non-finite input counts as silence. Parameters: `ceiling` (−24 to 0 dB),
`release` (1 ms to 2 s, 100 ms), `gain` (±24 dB). L is fixed at `prepare`
and reported as the latency and the tail.

## Why

- Ticket 27 found that sfizz's `limiter` has no look-ahead (a chord reached
  1.17) and put a compressor per node in its place; the sum of several
  nodes still needs one limiter that guarantees the ceiling.
- The engine reads a node's latency once, after `prepare` (graph-001); a
  look-ahead that changes later would break the alignment of parallel
  paths. 1.5 ms (72 frames at 48 kHz) is small against the output latency
  of the devices.
- −1 dBFS leaves room for the inter-sample peaks a sample-peak limiter
  cannot see.

## Consequences

- Live events — the on-screen keys, MIDI with immediate timing — sound
  1.5 ms later through the limiter; scheduled events are pre-delivered by
  it. `graph.outputLatency` and `graph.latencyOf(limiter)` report it.
