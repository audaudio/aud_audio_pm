## Decision

The app's engine creates the master chain `delay`
(`aud.effects.stereo_delay`) → `reverb` (`aud.effects.plate`) → `limiter`
(`aud.effects.limiter`) → output when it starts; every channel node and
the node `external` connect into `delay`, where they sum. The limiter keeps
its defaults: ceiling −1 dBFS, release 100 ms, look-ahead 1.5 ms. Every
preset of `content/sfz/index.json` carries `effects` — the parameters of
the delay and the reverb by their ids —, which
`tool/convert-content-bundle.js` writes from one table of defaults and
per-preset values; the engine applies
them, ramped over 50 ms, after the preset loaded, and a failed load brings
back the previous preset with its effects. The defaults follow the legacy
"medium" settings: delay 0.4 s, feedback 0.15, mix 0.06, ping-pong; reverb
pre-delay 20 ms, decay 0.55 (about 2 s), damping 0.3, bandwidth 0.9995,
mix 0.15. The E-Piano gets more delay, the organ less of both, the strings
more and longer reverb, the vibraphone a little more of both. The
per-node limiters and the gain effect of the SFZ files stay.

## Why

- Dense chords reached +7.1 to +7.6 dBFS on the sum without the chain; the
  limiter holds the ceiling for every preset (see the measurements of
  ticket 28).
- The legacy app ran Apple's reverb and delay on iOS only and off by
  default; the new chain runs on both platforms with moderate values, which
  presets can raise or lower.
- Values in `index.json`, written by the converter, survive a new
  conversion and need no format of their own; a missing field falls back to
  the defaults in the app.
- Delay before reverb reverberates the echoes; the limiter last guards the
  output.

## Consequences

- Live notes sound 1.5 ms later (dsp-003).
- The app depends on `aud_dsp_effects`.
