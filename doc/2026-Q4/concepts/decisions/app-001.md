## Decision

`tool/convert-content-bundle.js` in `audanika_app` turns the legacy content
package once into `content/sfz`, which is committed; the legacy bundle
leaves the app. Per sample set it writes a folder with the WAVs and an
`instrument.sfz`; per preset of the sound group one `channel-<n>.sfz` per
mapped MIDI channel and an `external.sfz`, listed in `index.json`. The
files reproduce the legacy engine: every note plays the nearest root (a
tie takes the higher one), a transposition shifts the key ranges instead
of the pitch, loops keep their inclusive end, the velocity curve
(−16 dB at 0 to 0 dB at 100, linear in dB) becomes `amp_velcurve_N`, CC 7
follows the legacy table with a 0 dB start, attack and release are linear
through a flex envelope (`eg1_ampeg=1`; `ampeg_*` stay for the node's tail
estimate), and the level matches the legacy iOS output. In place of the
legacy master limiter every file limits its node at −0.5 dBFS with sfizz's
compressor (attack 0, ratio 50) followed by a gain effect that restores the
7.35 dB sfizz takes off after the effects; sfizz's own `limiter` has no
look-ahead and let a dense chord reach 1.17.
The reverb presets, the sine fallback, the play-mode variants and the
JavaScript sampler scripts (never active) are not converted.

## Why

- The user wants the app to sound as before; a converter checked against
  the legacy C++ reproduces it measurably (release −6.02 dB after 1 s of
  2 s, a piano note at velocity 76 peaks at −15.88 dBFS against −15.87).
- sfizz ignores `lochan`/`hichan` and the sampler node drops the MIDI
  channel, so a preset that maps three channels needs three instruments.
- sfizz releases exponentially; the legacy engine linearly. With `ampeg`
  the piano's 2 s tail would be at −39 dB after 1 s instead of −6 dB.
- Rebuilding once and committing the result keeps scope-002: no legacy
  format is a contract, the SFZ files are the instruments from now on.
