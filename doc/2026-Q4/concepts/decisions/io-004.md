## Decision

On macOS `aud_audio_io` lists the devices of the Core Audio HAL — UID,
name, route from the transport type and the data source, channels per
direction, nominal rates — and opens them with miniaudio's Core Audio
backend, always by UID: a stream without a device id opens the current
default device, and when the default changes the backend reports the device
as lost, so that the stream's worker reopens it on the new default, as a
stream follows the route on iOS. miniaudio never switches devices or the
device's rate on its own. Listeners on a queue of the backend watch the
device list, the defaults and, per device a stream uses, its presence,
nominal rate, channels, data source and processor overloads: a gone device,
a new rate or a new channel count reopen the stream, a new data source
(headphones in the built-in jack) reports a route change, an overload
counts as an xrun. Host times are estimates as on iOS: the callback time
plus the block and the device's latency, safety offset and stream latency.
The hook builds the backend for macOS only; the native tests build the C++
files alone and keep the null device.

## Why

- miniaudio is already vendored for iOS (io-001), and the HAL gives what
  AVAudioSession gives on iOS: devices, routes and their changes.
- Following the default by reopening keeps one recovery path for every
  platform; miniaudio's own switching would change rate and channels behind
  the stream's back.
- Measured on the development Mac (ticket 37): the app's sound at 48 kHz
  and 256 frames, 7.4 ms output latency, 10.6 ms from command to sound,
  100 notes without an xrun, at most 0.14 ms of render time per block.
