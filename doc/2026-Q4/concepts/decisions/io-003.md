# io-003: The audio thread runs on the fast cores of Android devices

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Canonical source: tickets/2026-10-13-30-render-the-sampler-voices-faster-on-the-big-cores.md
  (open questions 2 and 3, answered by Gabriel Gatzsche on 2026-10-13:
  "pinning accepted on Android 10 and 11", "more battery drain accepted";
  decisions 3 and 4 of the plan review, confirmed on 2026-10-14)
- Refines: [io-002](io-002.md) — its open work "the audio thread on the
  small cores" of the Galaxy Tab S4 (ticket 28)
- Open work: the effect of the performance hint on a device with Android
  12 or later (no reference device runs it; the emulator only proves the
  path); the workload hint (`notifyWorkloadSpike`, Android 16) for the
  bursts of note-ons

## Decision

The Oboe backend of `aud_audio_io` moves its callback thread onto the fast
cores — the cores whose highest clock (`cpuinfo_max_freq`) lies above the
slowest one: the big cluster of two, the big and prime cores of three,
where one prime core alone would leave the thread no room to move.

- **Android 12 and later**: a performance hint session (ADPF) of the
  callback thread, opened from its first callback, as Oboe opens its own,
  since the session needs the thread's id. The target is half of a burst,
  and every callback reports its work, scaled to a burst. Oboe's own hint
  is not used: it aims at the whole burst, so the system would slow the
  thread until each callback fills its period, and the voice governor of
  lifecycle-003, which aims the mean render at 75 % of the block, would
  answer with fewer voices; its report also takes a mutex on the audio
  thread.
- **Android 10 and 11**, and wherever the system makes no session: the
  callback thread binds itself to the fast cores (`sched_setaffinity`) in
  its first callback. Android resets the affinity of a thread when it moves
  the app between cpusets — to the background and back —, so a check about
  once a second binds it again. When the system refuses, the thread runs
  where the system places it.
- **One cluster** (the emulator, many small devices): nothing changes.
- **iOS**: nothing changes. The engine renders serially on Core Audio's IO
  thread (sched-001), which the system already schedules in the device's
  audio workgroup; a workgroup matters only for helper threads.

`AudIoCounters` (API 3) reports the placement (`system`, `hint`,
`affinity`, `refused`), the fast cores, the affinity set again after a
reset, the refusals, and the core of about one callback a second, from
which `fastCpuShare` follows.

## Why

- On the Galaxy Tab S4 (Android 10) the callback thread ran on the small
  in-order cores in every sample of ticket 28 and in 99.3 to 99.7 % of those
  of ticket 30. A voice of the app costs 29.2 µs per block of 180 frames
  there and 12.2 µs on the big cores
  (the native bench of ticket 30) — 2.4 times as much for a clock only
  1.24 times lower.
- Measured on the Galaxy Tab S4 with the voice loop of 0.3.2: the thread
  ran on the big cores in every sample (99.5 % of the callbacks the backend
  sampled; the first ones run before the binding), the share of the block
  without voices fell from 36.2 to 17.8 % and the share of a voice from
  0.84 to 0.60 %; the voices at a mean of 75 % rose from 46 to 96, and
  without a budget the tablet played 120 voices at 0.79 without an xrun
  instead of 87 at 1.36 with 1157.
- Qualcomm's core control isolates idle big cores, and the kernel leaves
  them out of the affinity it returns; an exact comparison bound the thread
  anew every second, so the check accepts a mask within the fast cores.
- The performance hint is the system's own way from Android 12 and keeps
  the power policy with the system; an affinity is the only lever below.
  On the Android emulator (Android 16) the session opens.
- The user accepted the battery drain of the big cores while the app plays
  (open question 3 of ticket 30). With the faster voices of sampler-009 the
  stress test drained the tablet less than before: 101 instead of 231 mAh
  in ten minutes, as the system computes it, the CPU 0.7 instead of 2.6 °C
  warmer.
